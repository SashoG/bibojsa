# Referee-style review — BIBO GVM/JSA manuscript (draft v0.2)

Reviewed 2026-09-04 against `manuscript/manuscript_oe.tex`, `manuscript/references.bib`,
the `bibojsa` package and `bibojsa/survey/*.csv`.
Full formatted version: published as a Claude artifact ("BIBO Manuscript Referee Report").

## Verdict

Numerics are sound; the survey design is not. Do not submit as drafted.
Three blocking issues, all fixable; two of the fixes make the paper stronger.

---

## B1 — the 2.6–2.9 um idler is outside BIBO's transparency window  [BLOCKING]

Every point in Table 1 puts the heralding idler at 2.6–2.9 um. BIBO's published
transmission range is **286–2500 nm** (Hellwig/Liebertz/Bohaty, J. Appl. Phys. 88, 240
(2000); patent US6264858B1; all vendor datasheets). The 3.09 um figure in the manuscript
is the *Sellmeier validity range*, not transparency — the manuscript conflates them.

Worse: direct index measurements on BIBO stop near 2.3–2.4 um. The extension of the
Miyata–Umemura–Kato fit to 3.083 um rests on OPO phase-matching data in which the
long-wave idler was inferred from energy conservation, never measured. The ~3060 nm
idler in LPR2010 is a calculated complement. No published BIBO absorption coefficient
exists in 2.5–3.0 um; no measured BIBO output beyond 2.5 um exists at all.

FIX: move the headline to a configuration with both photons inside 2.5 um (see B2);
add a paragraph separating Sellmeier validity from transparency; keep the mid-IR points
as an explicitly flagged extrapolation.

## B2 — the pump grid sets the conclusion, and hides BIBO's best points  [BLOCKING]

Sec. 3 claims "*all* useful GVM operating points of BIBO live in the x–z plane with
775–810 nm pumps". The evidence supports only a claim about {405, 515, 775, 780, 800,
810} nm. Sweeping the pump continuously along the degenerate line of the *same* oeo x–z
interaction, using the unmodified `bibojsa` package, gives all three special orientations
— none of them in the scanned grid. The 775–810 nm band sits in the flat dead zone
between the 0 deg and 45 deg crossings, which is exactly why only strongly nondegenerate
crossings with mid-IR idlers were found there.

## B3 — the closest prior art is uncited  [BLOCKING]

**Jin, Cai, Ding, Mei, Deng, Shimizu, Zhou, "Spectrally uncorrelated biphotons generated
from 'the family of BBO crystal'", Quantum Engineering 2, e38 (2020); arXiv:1912.02497.**
Surveys 14 borates incl. BiBO, x–z plane, type-II, degenerate. Their table: GVM1 at
687 nm pump (1374 nm, 46.0 deg, purity ~0.97), GVM2 at 1119 nm (2238 nm, 46.0 deg, ~0.97),
GVM3 at 875 nm (1750 nm, 43.8 deg, ~0.82). These are, within Sellmeier-choice offsets,
exactly the three points the grid missed. Your code reproduces them — say so, it is a
genuine cross-validation.

Also missing entirely: any prior BIBO photon-pair work. At minimum
**Hentschel, Hubel, Poppe, Zeilinger, Opt. Express 19, 20420 (2011)** (non-collinear
type-II BIBO polarization-entangled source — published in the target journal);
**Branczyk et al., Opt. Express 19, 55 (2011)** and **Grice, U'Ren, Walmsley, PRA 64,
063815 (2001)** for the 0.81 ceiling; and the survey genre (Jin 2019 KDP isomorphs;
Cai 2023, 22 mid-IR crystals; the Lee/Kim MDPI series).

## Verified as correct

- Point-group-2 deff expressions, re-derived independently from Voigt contraction:
  deff^oeo = d12 cos(theta), deff^eeo = -d14 sin(2 theta) in x–z; Eq. (2) y–z asymmetry
  incl. 2.39 / 3.64 pm/V at the mirror angles; identically-vanishing y–z oeo.
  b || dielectric x is the correct assignment for BIBO.
- Coefficient set {d11=2.53, d12=3.2, d13=-1.76, d14=1.66} pm/V is the exact Kleinman
  average of Ghotbi & Ebrahim-Zadeh's dielectric-frame tensor (2.93/3.48, -1.93/-1.58,
  1.63/1.67/1.67). Defensible — say so. Note the underlying Kleinman violations are
  17–20%, larger than the quoted error bars.
- Walk-off: 83.12 mrad for the e-polarized signal at point #45, matching Table 1.
- JSA construction: sinc(dk L/2) exp(i dk L/2) with the phase kept, dk evaluated exactly,
  uniform omega grids (so the unweighted SVD is legitimate), heralding filter applied as
  an amplitude filter before decomposition. All correct.
- Convergence treatment is better than the field's norm — keep it prominent.

## M1 — you are burying a much stronger result: 0.95, not 0.87  [MAJOR]

`survey_points.py` computes the 1x filtered purity and the manuscript never reports it.
At point #45 a Gaussian heralding filter of intensity FWHM equal to the pump's gives
**P = 0.950 with 70% of pairs transmitted**, converged at every window scale and both
grid sizes. Intermediate: 1.5x -> 0.925 at 81%; 2x -> 0.903 at 87%; 3x -> 0.870 at 93%.
Domain-engineering papers quote exactly this unfiltered/gently-filtered pairing; 0.95 at
70% transmission stands next to them, 0.87 does not.
FIX: report purity vs filter width with transmitted fraction on the same axis.

## M2 — "competitive with periodically poled KTP" will not survive  [MAJOR]

0.811 is not a BIBO result: it is the universal sinc-sidelobe ceiling for any
symmetric-GVM source with a Gaussian pump (Branczyk 2011, from Grice 2001), reproduced
at 0.82–0.83 in PPKTP, doped PPLN, the BBO family, and now twice in your own data
(0.811 nondegenerate, 0.810 degenerate). That agreement validates your code — present it
that way. But scope the PPKTP comparison: Graffitti 2018 measured 90.7% unfiltered,
Pickston 2021 95.3%, Cai 2025 >99.4%. Honest claim: *competitive with unapodized PPKTP,
at the sinc ceiling that bounds all bulk birefringent sources, without poling*.
On brightness, state that 2.5 pm/V is comparable to type-II PPKTP's (2/pi)*d24, and that
83 mrad of walk-off gives most of it back.

## M3 — heralding efficiency is the missing figure of merit  [MAJOR]

With the herald at 2.8 um the best published detection is upconversion at ~10–15% module
efficiency (Mancinelli et al., Nat. Commun. 8, 15184 — twins at 2.89/3.34 um). Mid-IR
SNSPD *system* efficiencies at 2.8 um are essentially unpublished, and blackbody
background dominates dark counts. Moving to 1.36 or 1.74 um removes the objection.

## Minor

- **m1** Eq. (3) is the ridge slope d(omega_i)/d(omega_s), i.e. the (omega_s, omega_i)
  plane with signal horizontal. The text says "(omega_i, omega_s) plane" and
  `fig5_jsa_maps.tex` plots idler on x. In the figure's axes the slope is the reciprocal,
  so the 0 deg and 90 deg labels swap. 45 deg is self-inverse, so no EPM result changes,
  but the ooe 0/90 crossings are mislabelled relative to what is plotted.
- **m1b** Fig. 5 uses wavelength axes: at point #45 a true 45 deg frequency-space ridge
  appears at arctan(lam_s^2/lam_i^2) ~ 9 deg. Plot JSAs in angular-frequency detuning.
- **m2** "not window-convergent" overstates it — the JSA is square-integrable, so the
  limit exists; the 1/x sinc tails just make convergence slow and the Sellmeier clip
  prevents reaching it. (#45 drifts 0.8194 -> 0.8082 with the idler window pinned at
  3.05 um throughout; #42 drifts 0.8065 -> 0.7701.) Also: the abstract leads with the
  unfiltered 0.81 while Sec. 4.3 says not to trust it and Table 1's caption calls the
  same points "window-converged". Pick one story.
- **m3** Sec. 4.1 says "P = 0.175 at the best scanned combination (2 mm, 40 nm pump
  FWHM)". The bandwidth grid is {1,2,4,8,16,32} nm and `survey_summary.csv` gives 0.1558
  at 2 mm / 32 nm, which is what Table 1 prints. Stale text.
- **m4** "triple-GVM point" is loose: at the crossing Delta_31 = -Delta_32 ~ 2.4 fs/mm —
  equal and opposite, not zero. "All three group delays matched to <0.15%" is accurate;
  make that the primary phrasing.
- **m5** L_eff = w0/rho is a conservative variant of the aperture length sqrt(pi)*w0/rho
  (1.28 mm, not 0.72). State the convention.
- **m6** Brightness proxy deff^2 min(L,L_eff)^2 assumes fixed collection bandwidth; since
  the purity optimum ties bandwidth to L, the integrated rate scales closer to L. Define
  the assumptions.
- **m7** `polyanskiy2024` is in the bib and never cited.

## Novelty

The numerical GVM/purity survey is a crowded genre since 2017 (Laudenbach PRApplied 2017;
McCracken JOSA B 2018; Jin PRApplied 2019 and Quantum Eng. 2020; Wei doped PPLN; Cai 2023;
Lee/Kim in MDPI Crystals and Applied Sciences). BIBO is already inside that net.
What survives, if foregrounded:

1. **Full monoclinic rigor** — every other survey in the genre uses orthorhombic or
   uniaxial crystals where deff is even or odd in theta. Point-group-2 handling
   (octant inequivalence, the eight odd-in-x elements, symmetry-verified expressions,
   2.39 vs 3.64 pm/V) is new to this genre and generalizes to all monoclinic crystals.
2. **Nondegenerate loci** rather than degenerate points only — a real extension over
   Jin 2020, *provided* lambda_p is also swept continuously (B2).
3. **Convergence honesty** — that unfiltered purities in this literature are quoted with
   no spectral-window convention, quantified. Currently buried in Sec. 4.3.
4. **The Sellmeier-slope cautionary result** (Kato–Takaoka n_y misclassifies the KTP GVM
   regime despite accurate absolute index) — currently a throwaway sentence.
5. **The 1.34 um reinterpretation** as a near-triple-GVM brightness configuration rather
   than a purity one. Clean, correct, quotable. Keep it.

## Optics Express fit

Scope is fine — OE publishes numerical/theoretical design studies in this area (Branczyk
19, 55; Wei's doped-PPLN theory paper; Pickston 29, 6991; Kim 33, 22087), and single
authorship is not a criterion. But OE's review criteria say the journal "emphasizes both
novelty and impact" and that *incremental work is likely to be rejected*, and the closest
analogue (the BMBF purity study) landed in MDPI Crystals.

As drafted this gets desk-rejected or bounced at first review, mainly on B3 and B1.
Revised as above — continuous-pump landscape, transparency-respecting headline at 1.36 or
1.74 um, 0.95 gently-filtered purity, monoclinic rigor as the hook, explicit positioning
against Jin 2020 — it clears the bar. Fallbacks closer to the current form: JOSA B or
Applied Physics B. MDPI Crystals would certainly take it but is a step down.

Structural suggestion: the paper reads as "here is a new BIBO source". It is stronger as
"here is how to survey a monoclinic biaxial crystal for spectral purity, with BIBO as the
worked case, including the three ways this analysis goes wrong if you are careless" —
pump-grid artifacts, window truncation, and Sellmeier slope.
