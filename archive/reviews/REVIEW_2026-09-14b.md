# Second review of manuscript v0.4 — physics, novelty, presentation, fit for Optics Express

Reviewed: `manuscript/manuscript_oe.tex` (v0.4, 14 Sep 2026, 17 template pages, ~9 600 words, 199-word abstract), the compiled PDF, `references.bib`, and the survey CSVs behind Figs. 1–6 and Table 2. Numerics were re-verified in the 14 Sep review and are not re-derived here; every number quoted below was checked against the CSVs in `bibojsa/survey/`.

## Verdict

**Suitable for Optics Express in scope; novelty is adequate but currently mis-sold; not ready to submit as is.** OE publishes single-author numerical source-design papers of exactly this kind and its stated bar is "simulation claims demonstrated, validated against a known limit" — v0.4 clears that bar comfortably (KDP/PPKTP validation, three-fit sensitivity, window-tail law with residuals, regression suite). What it does not yet clear is OE's other stated criterion, "incremental work is likely to be rejected": the methodological claim the paper leads with ("we promote λ_p to a survey variable; existing surveys use degeneracy or a fixed pump list") is **not accurate** against two cited/uncited papers, and a referee from the Jin, Laudenbach or K. J. Lee groups will say so in the first paragraph of their report. Reframed around what is actually new (below), the paper is a solid OE research article. If you want a lower-risk venue with no reframing, JOSA B is the natural home for this genre; Optics Continuum is the safe fallback.

Estimated outcome at OE after the fixes below: major-or-minor revision, then accept. Submitted as is: a real chance of "incremental, reject" from one referee.

## 1. Novelty — what is new and what is not

**Not new, and the paper currently claims it is.**

- *Continuous-pump, nondegenerate GVM loci.* Laudenbach et al. 2017 (ref. laudenbach2017), Figs. 5–14, plot signal wavelength versus **continuous** pump wavelength for every configuration with K ≤ 1.01 in five poled KTP isomorphs, type-0 and type-II, and Figs. 15–16 plot degenerate purity versus pump wavelength. That is the level-curve idea, for poled crystals, published nine years ago in a paper the manuscript cites. The sentence in Sec. 1 "These surveys evaluate the GVM conditions either at frequency degeneracy alone or at a fixed short list of pump wavelengths" is false for that reference.
- *Bulk birefringent GVM survey with walk-off and continuous tunability.* Kim, Lee & Lee, "Numerical investigation of high-purity polarization-entangled photon-pair generation in non-poled KTP isomorphs", Appl. Sci. 11, 565 (2021) — not cited — does type-II EPM in five **non-poled** KTP isomorphs over full spherical propagation angles (not just principal planes), tunable over 1884–2068 nm, with d_eff and walk-off angles and a beam-displacement estimate. It is the closest antecedent to Secs. 3–4.6 and must be cited and positioned against.
- *BIBO degenerate points and purities* — Jin 2020, already handled correctly in v0.4.

**Actually new, and worth leading with.**

1. The complete principal-plane GVM landscape of a **monoclinic** crystal with the point-group-2 d_eff, including the negative results (y–z carries the largest d_eff and no usable GVM; x–y nothing; x–z eoe too weak) and the nondegenerate type-II loci of BIBO (point B). Nobody has published this for BIBO and the octant-inequivalence treatment transfers to other point-group-2 hosts.
2. The window law P(W) = P_∞ + C/W with C set by the GVM class (Sec. 4.5). I found no prior statement of the 1/W scaling or of (P_∞, C) as the quantities to report; Graffitti 2018 made the truncation point qualitatively. This is the paper's most transferable result — it changes how every survey in the genre should quote its headline number — and the abstract should say so in one sentence rather than burying it after the BIBO numbers.
3. The type-II argument of Sec. 3.2 **extended to nondegenerate type-I** (see P1 below — this is currently missing and is the best cheap addition to the paper).
4. The walk-off-matched brightness line B = π d²/ρ² independent of L, and d_eff/ρ as the figure of merit for bulk birefringent sources (Sec. 4.6). Simple, but I have not seen it stated for SPDC.

**Recommended reframing.** Replace the "first feature is methodological" paragraph of Sec. 1 with: level-curve tracing of the GVM conditions against a continuous pump is established for periodically poled crystals, where the poling period is the third free parameter (laudenbach2017), and has been applied to bulk EPM in the KTP isomorphs (Kim 2021); here the free parameter is the birefringent cut angle of a biaxial monoclinic crystal, the three loci are traced as exact θ_PM level curves rather than a purity threshold, and all eight principal-plane interactions are covered. Then state the three real novelties. Prism requires a separate novelty statement for OE; draft it from items 1–4 above, not from the pump-list claim.

## 2. Physics

**P1 (add — strengthens the paper).** Fig. 4 shows the nondegenerate type-I loci (ooe, eeo) at purities of 0.03–0.34 (ooe) and 0.13–0.89 (eeo), and the text never says why. The reason is in the data and it generalizes Sec. 3.2: for two daughters on the same eigenbranch, g_0 − g_90 = k_i' − k_s' is only the dispersion difference between λ_s and λ_i, so the three type-I loci can never separate by more than |k_s' − k_i'| and max(|Δ_31|, |Δ_32|) ≤ |k_s' − k_i'|. In BIBO that bound is 0.4–13 fs/mm along the whole ooe family and 0.5–54 fs/mm along eeo, against 89–169 fs/mm for oeo 0° (from `landscape_curves.csv`; visible in Fig. 3 right and in the near-coincidence of the three curves in Fig. 1 middle/right). Every nondegenerate type-I GVM point is therefore *near-triple*: the phase-matching bandwidth is too broad for any pump, and the JSA is pump-dominated. Two sentences in Sec. 3.2 (retitle it "Why GVM engineering needs a type-II interaction" — drop "degenerate") plus one clause in the Fig. 4 caption close the gap and turn a remark a referee called elementary into a statement with a quantitative bound. The one exception, eeo 0° at 836 nm reaching 0.89 with a 2.92-µm idler, is worth a parenthesis as the limiting case of the bound.

**P2 (wrong sentence).** Sec. 4.5: "the weight inside a window of half-width W therefore falls only as 1/W" is backwards. ∫ dx/x² converges; the tail weight *included* by the window approaches its limit as ε_∞ − c/W and the *excluded* weight falls as 1/W. Since the included weight is what lowers P, P(W) = (1 − 2ε_∞) + 2c/W, which is Eq. (6) with C > 0 — larger window, more tail, lower purity. Reword; the conclusion is right, the mechanism sentence is not.

**P3 (definition of σ is off by √2).** Sec. 4.5 defines σ as "the 1/e half-width of the pump amplitude in angular frequency". The numbers say otherwise: at A the 8-nm pump gives an intensity FWHM of 32.4 rad/ps and `window_tail.csv` has σ = 19.44 rad/ps, ratio 1.666 = 2√ln 2, so σ is the 1/e half-width of the pump **intensity** (equivalently, the amplitude is exp(−δω²/2σ²)). The amplitude 1/e half-width would be 27.5 rad/ps. All nm conversions in Table 2 are consistent with σ = 19.44, so only the sentence is wrong — but C is quoted "per σ" and a referee who checks will find the factor.

**P4 (Jin comparison — make it a check instead of a shrug).** Sec. 4.1 says Jin et al. quote 0.97 "without stating a length or bandwidth". Their figure captions state L and Δλ for every JSA shown; all their purities use Δλ ≈ 3 nm and L between 4 and 50 mm, and the one BIBO JSA they show (GVM3/EPM, Fig. 3f) is L = 4 mm, Δλ = 3 nm, P = 0.82 — which matches your D within 0.01. For the 0° point, a 3-nm pump maps through your 1/L optimum-bandwidth scaling (8 nm at 10 mm → 4 nm at 30 mm) to L ≈ 25–30 mm, where you get 0.967. So the sentence should read: their fixed 3-nm pump corresponds to a ≈30-mm crystal on our length scan, and our 30-mm value reproduces their 0.97; their EPM value at 4 mm/3 nm agrees with D. That is a validation, not a discrepancy.

**P5 (window-tail fit for B and C rests on three points).** `window_tail.csv`: B and C have `clipped = 1` at 16σ and 24σ (idler side hits the Sellmeier limit), so P_∞ and C for them are a 2-parameter fit to 3 points (`fit_points = 3`, residual trivially 0). Sec. 4.5 says "to within 10⁻⁴ over the whole range" at "every sinc-limited point" — true for A and D (5 points, residuals 2×10⁻⁵ and 8×10⁻⁵), not demonstrated for B and C. Say so in one clause, or run B and C on a signal-side-only widening.

**P6 (detector statement for B).** Sec. 5.3(ii): "Points A and B, at 1.36–1.92 µm, avoid this problem altogether." At B the filtered/heralding arm throughout the paper is the idler, at 1.924 µm, beyond standard InGaAs (~1.7 µm); heralding there needs extended-InGaAs or an SNSPD. Either herald B on the 1.156-µm photon (then the filter analysis for B should be redone on the signal arm) or qualify the sentence. A is fine.

**P7 (brightness claim overreaches its proxy).** Sec. 4.3 concludes "an order of magnitude more spectral brightness" for the triple-GVM family "by Eq. (7)", and Sec. 4.6 repeats "a factor of roughly fifteen in B". Eq. (7) is a plane-wave proxy that you yourself qualify (no Boyd–Kleinman, walking wave is the pump at E). Keep the figure of merit d_eff/ρ and its factor of four; downgrade "order of magnitude more spectral brightness" to "a proxy gain of ~15 in B, to be confirmed by a focused-beam calculation". Also note that at E the emission bandwidth is not 1/L-limited, so "at fixed collected bandwidth" is doing real work in that comparison — say it.

**P8 (small inconsistencies).**
- Sec. 4.3: E's JSA is "five times wider in frequency than any other panel"; the Fig. 5 caption says "four times wider than D's and seven times wider than A's". Axes: E ±400, D ±100, A ±60. Fix the text.
- Sec. 2.5 KDP validation quotes P = 0.92 for Mosley with no collection band — your own Sec. 4.5 rule. Add the band (or P_∞).
- Abstract: "C four to five times smaller" — ratios from Table 2 are 5.4 (A), 5.6 (B), 3.8 (C). "Four to six" or "four to five" with C rounded — either, but be consistent with the table.
- "Ti:sapphire-accessible member" for B implies A (682 nm) is not; 682 nm is at the short edge of standard Ti:sapphire tuning but reachable. Say "comfortably inside the Ti:sapphire range" for B.
- Conclusions: "the validated **open** computational package accompany this paper" vs. Data availability: "available from the author upon reasonable request". One or the other. A Zenodo DOI for `bibojsa` resolves it, costs an hour, and pre-empts the standard OE referee request for code in a computational paper.
- Table 2, E/F/R: P_90/P_70 rows lack the filter FWHM in nm that A–D have (the values exist in `window_tail.csv`: E 649/317, F 495/239, R 230/96 nm).

**Physics that holds up (checked).** Type-I degeneracy remark; sign/region assignment of the four x–z interactions against Ω = 26.3°; every d_eff in Table 2 recomputed from the Table-1 expressions (2.22/2.40/2.22/2.31/3.14/1.56/2.47 ✓); walk-off 82–85 mrad from n_x, n_z at 45° and 30.7 mrad at 11° (∝ sin 2θ) ✓; first sinc zero 3.71 rad/ps at A and 21.5 rad/ps along the antidiagonal at D ✓; L_a = 1.25 mm at 60 µm and matched waist 1.45 mm at 30 mm ✓; 90 %-flux filter width 10.7 nm at A is consistent with a 3.3-nm sinc² idler marginal ✓; e⁻¹ transmission through 10 mm at 1 cm⁻¹ ✓; the absorption/heralding-loss framing of Sec. 5.1 is now correct.

## 3. Presentation

**Abstract: 199 words against Optica's "approximately 100".** This alone will bounce at Prism intake or draw an editor note. Cut to ~110 words: one sentence on the landscape (three families in oeo, nothing outside x–z, type-I pinned), one on the headline numbers (0.949 / 0.967 at 30 mm / 0.988 with a 90 % filter, both photons at 1.364 µm), one on the window law (P_∞ + C/W, C smaller for asymmetric GVM, the 0.81–0.83 EPM value is a band convention), one on point B. Drop "Numerical surveys usually fix a short list…" (see novelty) and the sensitivity/absorption/brightness list.

**Length.** 17 pages in the Optica template and ~9 600 words is long for a numerical survey; OE's base APC covers 15 published pages (third-party guide — confirm on the OE page-charge sheet). Targets, in order of payoff: move the d-tensor cross-check and finite-difference details of Sec. 2.1–2.2 to two sentences plus a supplement; merge Secs. 4.2 and 4.4 (both are about the EPM points and filtering); cut Sec. 5.2 by half — the "honest positioning" paragraph says the same thing three times; drop the sentence-level rhetoric that reads as replies to a referee ("That spread is itself the result worth quoting", "This is a redefinition rather than a rejection", "And a ceiling is what this is", "The honest positioning is"). A 13–14-page version loses nothing a referee needs.

**Title.** Eighteen words, two clauses. Something like "Group-velocity-matched photon pairs in monoclinic BiB₃O₆: the full phase-matching landscape and the band dependence of unfiltered purity" keeps both novelties; or shorter still, "Mapping group-velocity matching in monoclinic BiB₃O₆ for high-purity photon pairs".

**Figures.**
- Figs. 3 and 4: three panels in 0.86–0.94 linewidth gives ~4-cm panels with ~6-pt tick labels — below OE's readable minimum at print size. Either two rows, or drop the type-I panels of Fig. 4 into one combined panel once P1 explains them in the text.
- Fig. 1: the "A" label is clipped by the left axis of the oeo panel; "point B" as a legend entry with a hollow marker is odd — label the two B markers in the panel and drop the legend line. The near-coincidence of the three curves in the ooe/eeo panels is the visual evidence for P1 — say so in the caption.
- Fig. 4: caption should state the type-I panels' low values are the near-triple effect (P1); without that, the panels look like a failed calculation.
- Fig. 6: mark the 90 % and 70 % transmission abscissae, since Table 2 quotes exactly those two.
- Fig. 5: fine; consider showing F instead of R if R is dropped (below).

**Table 2 and point R.** The caption explains R as "singled out in an earlier version of this survey". Referees do not care about earlier versions. Either give R a reason to exist in this paper — it is the nondegenerate EPM member a conventional 800-nm Ti:sapphire pump list picks, and it demonstrates the absorption-band and truncation problems of Sec. 5.1 in one row — or remove the column. I would keep it, with that justification, and the caption shortened by half.

## 4. Suggested novelty statement (for Prism)

We report the first complete group-velocity-matching landscape of a monoclinic nonlinear crystal, BiB₃O₆, treating all eight principal-plane interactions with the full point-group-2 nonlinearity and tracing the three GVM conditions as continuous level curves in pump wavelength. The survey identifies the crystal's high-purity configurations (unfiltered heralded purity 0.949–0.967 at 1.364 µm without periodic poling, plus a Ti:sapphire-pumped 1.16/1.92-µm pair not previously reported), establishes that no usable GVM exists outside the x–z plane despite the larger nonlinearity there, and derives a bound showing that type-I GVM points are necessarily near-triple. Beyond BIBO, we show that every sinc-limited unfiltered purity depends on the collection band as P_∞ + C/W with a coefficient set by the GVM class, so that the widely quoted 0.81–0.83 symmetric-GVM value is a band convention; we propose that surveys report (P_∞, C).
