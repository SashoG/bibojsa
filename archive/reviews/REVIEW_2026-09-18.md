# Fourth review of the BIBO manuscript (v0.5.1) — novelty for Optics Express, results re-verified

Reviewed 18 September 2026: `manuscript/manuscript_oe.tex` v0.5.1 and its PDF (15 pages, 37 references, 122-word abstract), `references.bib`, the survey CSVs in `bibojsa/survey/`, the `bibojsa` package and its test suite, and the three earlier reviews (4 Sep, 14 Sep, 14 Sep b) plus the 18 Sep figure review. The question asked for this pass: is the work novel enough for Optics Express? To answer it I (i) ran a fresh prior-art search (about 45 queries, arXiv/journal full texts read where it mattered), (ii) had every headline number recomputed from scratch in an independent numpy/scipy implementation that does not import `bibojsa`, and (iii) re-read the manuscript as an OE referee would.

## 1. Verdict

**Publishable in Optics Express after a positioning revision; the numerics are sound; the novelty is real but narrower than the manuscript currently implies, and two of its four general claims have direct antecedents that are not cited.** Left as is, I put the chance of at least one "incremental — reject" referee report at roughly one in three; with the repositioning in Sec. 3 below it drops to the ordinary major/minor-revision path. If you want the lower-risk venue with the same text, JOSA B remains the natural home (McCracken–Graffitti–Fedrizzi 2018 is there); Physical Review Applied is where the two closest surveys (Laudenbach 2017, Jin 2019) went and would also take it. Purely numerical GVM surveys have appeared in OE itself (the Jin-group doped-PPLN survey, Opt. Express 29, 256 (2021)), so the genre is not a problem; the framing is.

What OE's own criteria say (review-criteria sheet, 2026): "novel papers must contain new information that has the potential to significantly impact the field"; "incremental work is likely to be rejected"; conclusions "supported by the data"; work "placed in proper context" with adequate references. The manuscript is strong on the second and third and currently weak on the fourth for two claims. Mechanics: abstract "approximately 100 words" (122 is tolerable); no page limit, but the 2026 charge is \$2,300 up to 15 published pages and \$145 per page beyond — v0.5.1 sits exactly on the boundary, so anything added must be paid for by a cut.

Nothing found in this pass changes a single number in the paper. Everything below is about what the numbers are claimed to mean.

## 2. Novelty audit, claim by claim

### C1 — The BIBO landscape (all eight principal-plane interactions, continuous level curves, negative results). **Novel as posed.**

No paper computes GVM loci, JSA or purity for BIBO beyond Jin et al. 2020 (Quantum Eng. 2, e38), and Jin 2020 has no BIBO follow-up: its 11 citing papers (Semantic Scholar, 2020–2025) contain nothing on BIBO or biaxial loci. The only 2026 BIBO quantum paper is experimental quantum imaging (Yoshimura et al., AIP Adv. 16, 065123) with no spectral content. Point B, the nondegenerate loci, the x–y / y–z negatives and the x–z eoe exclusion are all unpublished. Jin 2020 Table IV (nondegenerate configurations) lists BBO, CLBO, KABO, KBBF, RBBF, CBBF, BABF, LBO and LRB4 — BIBO is not in it, so "the nondegenerate loci ... have not been published" (Sec. 1) is correct.

**But one sentence over-reaches.** Sec. 1: "What BIBO adds to the genre is monoclinic symmetry." Jin 2020 Table I already contains three monoclinic crystals — LCB (point group 2, the same as BIBO, treated in the y–z plane), YCOB and GdCOB (point group m, y–z and x–y planes) — using simplified d_eff expressions (for BIBO itself, `d_eff(BiBO−xz) = d26 cos(θ+ρ)`). So monoclinic hosts are already in the genre; what is new here is the full point-group-2 d_eff for all three planes and all eight interactions, applied to the crystal Jin 2020 itself flagged as the priority ("it is promising to study GVM conditions in BiBO crystal in an arbitrary direction, since this crystal has the highest nonlinear coefficient in Table I"). Rewrite the sentence as: monoclinic crystals have entered the genre through single principal planes with simplified nonlinearities [jin2020]; here the full point-group-2 treatment is carried through every plane, which is what the y–z octant inequivalence requires. And note that Jin's own recommendation — arbitrary direction — is exactly what this paper does *not* do (see Sec. 5).

The 14b review's draft Prism statement said "the first complete GVM landscape of a monoclinic nonlinear crystal". Drop "monoclinic" from that phrase or qualify it ("first complete principal-plane GVM landscape of BiB₃O₆, and the first with the full point-group-2 nonlinearity"); a referee from Jin's group will otherwise cite LCB in the first paragraph.

### C2 — "Type-I bound", Eq. (6). **Partially anticipated in words; and Eq. (6) is an identity, not a bound.**

Two separate problems.

(a) *Prior statements.* That factorability requires the pump group slowness to lie between the daughters', which "is only possible for Type-II SPDC" (collinear), is in print in exactly those words: Rockovich, Wang & Gauthier, arXiv:2401.02319 (v2, 2024, still a preprint), citing Grice–U'Ren–Walmsley 2001 and Mosley 2008 NJP. Christ, Eckstein, Mosley & Silberhorn (Opt. Express 17, 3441 (2009)) state the co-propagating condition v_s < v_p < v_i and show that a *counter-propagating* type-I geometry escapes it, degenerate or not. Horoshko, Kolobov, Parigi & Treps (Opt. Lett. 49, 4078 (2024)) use the degenerate triple-GVM point of type-I MgO:PPLN at 775 → 1550 nm deliberately, for few-mode squeezing (K ≈ 2–3) — a published *use* of the configuration Sec. 4.3 calls "brightness, not purity", worth citing there. The nondegenerate corollary ("every nondegenerate type-I GVM point is near-triple") I could not find anywhere; that part is yours.

(b) *The inequality is vacuous.* At a θ_PM = 0° point Δ₃₂ = 0, so Δ₃₁ = k_s′ − k_i′ exactly; at 90° the mirror; at 45° Δ₃₁ = −Δ₃₂ = (k_s′ − k_i′)/2. Hence max(|Δ₃₁|,|Δ₃₂|) equals |k_s′ − k_i′| (0°, 90°) or half of it (45°) at *every* GVM point of *every* interaction type — the manuscript's own parenthesis "(equality at 0° and 90°, half of it at 45°)" says so. I checked all 1160 points in `landscape_curves.csv`: the ratio gvm_scale / |GD_s − GD_i| is 1.000 for every 0° and 90° family and 0.500 for every 45° family, type II included (x–z oeo 0°: 89–169 fs/mm = |k_s′ − k_i′|; oeo 45°: 24–73 = half of 49–145). A referee who notices will say "Eq. (6) is a tautology". The physics you want is one step further and stronger: **the group-delay scale of any GVM family is the daughters' group-delay mismatch (times ½ on the EPM family), and that mismatch is intra-branch chromatic dispersion for type I — bounded by the group-index variation of one branch between λ_s and λ_i, zero at the triple point, 0.4–54 fs/mm in BIBO — but inter-branch birefringence for type II, 37–189 fs/mm here (x–z oeo 90° reaches down to 37 fs/mm at its short-pump end; y–z oeo 90° up to 189).** State it as an identity plus that dichotomy, and Fig. 3 (right) is explained in full: it also says, for free, why the 45° family is "the weakest of the three" (Sec. 3.3) — it is simply the factor ½, not a property of BIBO's dispersion. Sec. 3.3 currently presents that as an empirical finding.

(c) *Scope the conclusion.* "Usefully separable pairs, degenerate or not, require a type-II interaction" holds for collinear, co-propagating bulk interactions in a single spatial mode. Say so in the sentence, and cite the three known escapes: counter-propagation [christ2009], non-collinear emission with focusing (Rockovich et al. predict P = 0.997 with 10-THz filters in 400-µm BBO), and modal dispersion in waveguides. Otherwise the sentence is falsifiable by citation.

### C3 — Window law P(W) = P_∞ + C/W. **Partially anticipated; the 1/W form, the coefficient ratio and the (P_∞, C) proposal are new; the framing must change.**

Two antecedents the manuscript does not cite for this point, both of which a referee will know:

- Graffitti, Kelly-Massicotte, Fedrizzi & Brańczyk, PRA 98, 053811 (2018) — cited in the paper only for the 0.83 value — devote a section to it. They define ζ, "the ratio between the spectral range used in the JSA calculation and the average PDC photon bandwidth", show purity versus ζ and versus grid resolution N (their Figs. 10–12), conclude "purity is significantly overestimated for small spectral ranges ζ < 10", and their Table I gives the converged maximum for a Gaussian pump with a sinc PMF as **0.80** — i.e. your P_∞ = 0.798 at D reproduces a published number. The manuscript's "band-independent limit 0.80" and Fig. 4 caption should cite this as agreement, not present it as new; Fig. 4's caption currently cites the same paper for 0.83; its Table I value is 0.80.
- Cai, Baek, Jin & Kaneda, Adv. Phys. Res. (2025), doi:10.1002/apxr.202500167 (arXiv:2501.14214): "If the spectral range is too small, unwanted peripheral lobes in JSA can be filtered out, and therefore, one can overestimate the purity"; they fix R = 10Δω, note that "R can also be a simulation of a photon collection bandwidth", and plot purity versus R for four poling schemes (their Fig. 7). That is a published purity-versus-window curve for a sinc PMF, from the group whose survey conventions you are criticising.

What remains yours, and it is enough: the explicit 1/W law with a fitted coefficient (neither paper gives a functional form), the finding that C is 4–6× smaller for asymmetric than symmetric GVM (neither compares GVM classes), the observation that the survey literature (Jin 2019/2020/2023, Laudenbach 2017 — confirmed: none states an integration range) quotes third-decimal purities without the band, and the proposal to report (P_∞, C). Rewrite Sec. 1's "the window is rarely stated" as "rarely stated in the survey literature [jin2019, jin2020, cai2023, laudenbach2017], although its effect on the computed purity is documented [graffitti2018pra, cai2025]", and open Sec. 4.4 by crediting both before stating what is added. Suggested one-liner for the abstract: "...depends on the collection band as P_∞ + C/W, the 1/W form and its GVM-class dependence being the new result".

### C4 — Walk-off figure of merit d_eff/ρ. **Not found for SPDC; conceptually adjacent to classical work.**

No SPDC paper proposes d_eff/ρ or the L-independent walk-off-matched line. The classical antecedents are the Boyd–Kleinman aperture length (standard) and Grechin's "integral criterion" for crystal selection including walk-off (Quantum Electron. 39, 2009 — abstract only readable). An optional sentence acknowledging that walk-off-weighted crystal FOMs exist for classical conversion would pre-empt a nonlinear-optics referee. Bennink's PRA 81, 053805 (2010) result that optimal collinear Gaussian-beam collection is nearly independent of crystal length is a *different* L-independence (focusing, no walk-off) and is worth contrasting in one clause, since a reader may conflate them.

One factual point to add, because it contradicts folklore: Jin 2020 writes "BiBO has higher nonlinear coefficients and a smaller spatial walk-off angle than the BBO crystal". At the x–z type-II GVM cuts (θ ≈ 41–46°) the e-wave walk-off is 82–85 mrad — larger than BBO's usual 55–70 mrad — because the x–z birefringence is ~9 %. The manuscript's numbers are right (independently reproduced); say explicitly that the small-walk-off reputation comes from other planes and does not apply to these configurations.

### C5 — Headline design points. **Novel as specific configurations; reconciliation with Jin's 0.97 is already handled correctly.**

No source proposes 682 nm → 2 × 1.364 µm or 722 nm → 1.156 + 1.924 µm in BIBO other than Jin's degenerate 687-nm point. Sec. 4.1's 1/L mapping of Jin's 3-nm/4–50-mm calculation onto 0.967 at 25–30 mm is the right reconciliation (done in v0.5). Where 0.95–0.97 sits in the bulk-crystal field in 2026: Jin's KDP-isomorph asymmetric points 0.98 (2019); Lee/Kim EPM points 0.995–0.997 only with pump filtering, 0.833 unfiltered; Kim et al. OE 33, 22087 (2025) is bulk KTP non-critical EPM whose "purity ~0.99" is *polarization-state* purity from tomography, not spectral — do not let a comparison with it into Sec. 5.2 without that caveat. Rockovich et al. (above) is the bulk non-poled competitor for unfiltered-ish purity at 0.97 and must be cited in the limitations because it is non-collinear.

## 4. Other findings of this pass (none numerical)

P1. Abstract: "the single type-II interaction carries three GVM families" — the x–z plane has two type-II interactions (oeo, eoe) and the y–z plane a third; say "the single *usable* type-II interaction". Also "nothing useful lies outside the x–z plane" reads as a whole-crystal statement; the survey is principal-plane only, so "in the other principal planes".

P2. Sec. 3.3: "the 45° family is the weakest of the three in this respect (24–73 fs/mm ...)" — this is the factor ½ of C2(b), not a finding; say so.

P3. Fig. 4 caption cites graffitti2018pra for P = 0.83; that paper gives 0.80 (converged). Cite Laudenbach 2017 / Jin 2020 for 0.82–0.83 and graffitti2018pra for the 0.80 limit.

P4. Data File 1 still carries the 2.50-µm transparency flag (`landscape_curves.csv`: last flagged-in idler 2.4979 µm, first flagged-out 2.5004 µm) while the text and Fig. 1 say 2.6 µm. P5. Sec. 4.5: the walk-off-matched line at A gives matched waists of 0.48–1.45 mm at 10–30 mm. An OE referee will ask what that means in absolute terms. The proxy argument is fine; an unanchored proxy is what "indicative rather than quantitative" invites.

## 5. What would move the paper from "solid survey" to "clearly OE"

In order of payoff per day of work.

1. **Out-of-principal-plane scan around the oeo families** (largest payoff; several days). Jin 2020 names arbitrary-direction BIBO as the open problem; Kim 2021 did the full sphere for KTP isomorphs. The manuscript's own argument — that monoclinic symmetry matters where the shortcuts fail — points off-plane. Even a single result (does d_eff/ρ improve, or does the 0° family become a two-parameter tunable surface, when φ ≠ 0 near A?) would answer the first question every referee will ask and would justify the title's "mapping". If not done, the abstract and conclusions must say "principal-plane" every time "landscape" appears, and Sec. 5.3 should state why principal planes were chosen (LPR2010 d_eff tables; walk-off compensation geometries live there).
2. **Focused-beam JSA with walk-off at point A** (several days; `sevilla2026` gives the machinery). Turns Sec. 4.5 from a proxy into a number and settles the "is 85 mrad fatal?" objection.
3. **Absolute pair-rate estimate at A** (half a day; P5 above).
4. **Recast Eq. (6) as the identity + dichotomy** (an hour; C2(b)). Costs nothing and converts a referee's "tautology" into the paper's cleanest general statement.

Any one of 1–3 plus item 4 and the repositioning in Sec. 2 would, in my judgement, remove the "incremental" risk at OE.

## 6. Suggested replacement text

**Sec. 1, "What BIBO adds" paragraph.** "Monoclinic crystals have entered this genre through single principal planes with simplified effective nonlinearities [jin2020: LCB, YCOB, GdCOB]. In point group 2 the effective nonlinearity in the plane normal to the two-fold axis is neither even nor odd in the polar angle and the two octants are inequivalent [petrov2010, hellwig2000], so deciding whether the y–z plane — where that inequivalence lives — offers anything requires the full point-group-2 treatment in every plane. We carry it through all eight principal-plane interactions of BIBO, the crystal that Ref. [jin2020] singles out for its nonlinearity; the published BIBO results [jin2020] treat the x–z plane at degeneracy only."

**Sec. 3.2, replacing Eq. (6) and the sentence before it.** "At any GVM point the group-delay scale is fixed by the daughters alone: max(|Δ₃₁|,|Δ₃₂|) = |k_s′ − k_i′| on the 0° and 90° families and |k_s′ − k_i′|/2 on the 45° family, for every interaction type. What distinguishes type I is what that mismatch *is*: for two daughters on one eigenbranch it is the chromatic group-index difference of that branch between λ_s and λ_i — zero at the triple point and, in BIBO, 0.4–13 fs/mm along the ooe families and 0.5–54 fs/mm along eeo — whereas for type II it is the birefringent difference between branches, 37–189 fs/mm here."

**Prism novelty statement (replaces the 14b draft).** "We report the complete principal-plane group-velocity-matching landscape of BiB₃O₆, the first for this crystal and the first to use the full point-group-2 nonlinearity in every plane, tracing the three GVM conditions as continuous level curves in pump wavelength for all eight interactions. The survey identifies BIBO's high-purity configurations without periodic poling (unfiltered heralded purity 0.949–0.967 at 1.364 µm; a Ti:sapphire-pumped 1.16/1.92-µm pair not previously reported), shows that no usable GVM exists in the other principal planes despite the larger nonlinearity there, and expresses the group-delay scale of every GVM family through the daughters' mismatch, which separates type-I (intra-branch, near-triple) from type-II (birefringent) configurations. Beyond BIBO, we show that the unfiltered purity of any sinc-limited source follows P_∞ + C/W in the collection half-width W, with C four to six times smaller for asymmetric than for symmetric GVM, extending earlier observations of window dependence [Graffitti 2018; Cai 2025] to a quantitative law, and propose that surveys report (P_∞, C)."
