# Referee-style review of manuscript v0.3

**Manuscript:** *Mapping the group-velocity-matching landscape of a monoclinic biaxial crystal: high-purity photon-pair configurations of BiB₃O₆* (manuscript_oe.tex, v0.3, September 2026; target: Optics Express)
**Review date:** 14 September 2026
**Method:** full read of the .tex and compiled PDF; independent re-derivation of every headline number with code written from scratch (analytic Sellmeier derivatives, own phase-matching / JSA / SVD routines — no bibojsa imports); audit of the bibojsa source; cross-check against LPR2010 (Petrov et al. 2010), Hentschel et al. 2011, Graffitti et al. 2018, Jin et al. 2020 (arXiv:1912.02497), Pickston et al. 2021.

---

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

The computational machinery is sound: every number I could recompute independently — phase-matching angles, group-delay mismatches, d_eff, walk-off, the three degenerate GVM points under all three Sellmeier fits, the two type-I triple-GVM points, the x–y and y–z negative results, the unfiltered purities at the stated windows, and the filtered purities — reproduces to the last quoted digit (Sec. 2). Two of the paper's central *claims*, however, do not survive scrutiny: (M1) the statement that BIBO is not transparent beyond 2.5 µm is contradicted by LPR2010 itself, which the paper cites as its primary source; and (M2) the claim that the asymmetric-GVM purities are "window-converged" and "publication-grade" is wrong — they drift as 1/W exactly like the EPM purities, only ~4× more slowly, and the headline P = 0.949 is ≈ 0.942 in the unbounded-collection limit. A third issue (M3) is positioning: the three degenerate points that carry the abstract are already published (Jin et al. 2020) and the manuscript contains a factually false sentence about the prior surveys. All three are fixable, and the fixes actually strengthen the paper, but the manuscript should not be submitted before they are made. My assessment for Optics Express, after the fixes, is "publishable with major revision" — see Sec. 6 for the novelty discussion.

## 3. Major issues (must be fixed before submission)

### M1. The transparency argument is factually wrong as written

Manuscript (Sec. 1, Sec. 5.1, Fig. 1 caption, Table 2, Conclusions): "the crystal's measured transmission window ends near 2.5 µm [hellwig2000]"; "we could find no published absorption coefficient for BIBO between 2.5 and 3.0 µm, nor any report of measured BIBO output beyond 2.5 µm"; Table 2 flags R's 2.805-µm idler as "no" for transmission.

LPR2010 — the paper's own primary reference — states (Sec. 2, Table 4 and text):
- transparency range at the **1 cm⁻¹ absorption level: 286–2600 nm** (ref. [82] there = Ghotbi et al., Opt. Express 14, 10621 (2006));
- "The absorption feature near 2400 nm is seen only in thick samples ... The same also holds for the **OH⁻ peak near 2800 nm** ... The transparency of BIBO extends up to **6250 nm** [Teng et al., J. Cryst. Growth 224, 280 (2001)], but the practical upper limit is determined by two-phonon absorption";
- Sec. 6.1: the visible BIBO SPOPO "tuning range of the idler is from **3060 to 999 nm**", and "the upper wavelength limit for the idler of 3060 nm is indicative of the good transmission of the thin BIBO crystal in this region";
- Fig. 42: a measured **cross-correlation trace of BIBO OPA idler pulses at 3050 nm**.

Hellwig et al. 2000 (your [hellwig2000]) is an *index* measurement to 2325 nm; it is not a transmission cutoff at 2.5 µm. So: BIBO output beyond 2.5 µm has been measured, repeatedly, by the very group you cite; there *is* a published absorption figure (1 cm⁻¹ at 2.6 µm); and the relevant physics between 2.5 and 3.1 µm is a weak, thickness-dependent absorption band (2.4 µm feature, OH⁻ near 2.8 µm), not opacity.

Consequences:
1. The abstract's "transparency limits that exclude the nominally best operating points", the Sec. 1 "we show that this flag removes several of the nominally best points", Sec. 5.1 in its entirety, the Fig. 1 dash-dotted line, the Table 2 "no" for R, and the Conclusions sentence all need rewriting.
2. The correct framing is *loss in a 10-mm crystal*: at α ≈ 1 cm⁻¹ a 10-mm crystal transmits ~37 % of the idler — this degrades **heralding efficiency**, not purity, and it is wavelength- and sample-dependent. The 678-nm member (idler 3.015 µm) and R (2.805 µm, essentially on the OH⁻ band) deserve a *loss* caveat, not exclusion. The idler for point B (1.924 µm) and C (2.234 µm) are unaffected either way.
3. The remaining valid point — that a Sellmeier validity range is not a transparency range, and that the 2.4–3.1 µm branch of the MUK fit is anchored on OPO idlers inferred from energy conservation — is worth keeping, but as one paragraph, correctly sourced (Ghotbi 2006 for the 1 cm⁻¹ figure, Teng 2001 for the far-IR edge, LPR2010 for the OH⁻ feature and the 3.06-µm idler).

This is the most damaging issue for a referee who knows BIBO: it is checkable in thirty seconds against LPR2010 and it undermines the credibility of the material-science framing.

### M2. The "window-converged" claim for the asymmetric points is incorrect

Manuscript Sec. 4.5: "At the asymmetric points the purity is window-converged: A varies over 0.94926–0.94970 across the full window range ... spreads below 8×10⁻⁴ ... Purities computed at asymmetric-GVM points ... are the numbers we consider publication-grade."

Your window-scale range s ∈ [0.7, 1.3] multiplies only the sinc-extent term in `adaptive_axes`; because the 3×FWHM pad dominates, the actual half-window at A varies only from ≈6.0σ to ≈6.8σ of the pump. Over a *real* window scan at fixed resolution (pixel 1.0 rad/ps, checked against 0.5 rad/ps; grids up to 2334²) I find:

| half-window (pump σ) | P(A) | P(B) | P(C) | P(D) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 0.9488 | 0.9439 | 0.9385 | 0.8328 |
| 8 | 0.9471 | 0.9424 | 0.9362 | 0.8241 |
| 12 | 0.9455 | 0.9408 | 0.9339 | 0.8153 |
| 16 | 0.9447 | 0.9401 | 0.9328 | 0.8108 |
| 24 | 0.9439 | 0.9397 | — | 0.8064 |
| **1/W extrapolation, W→∞** | **0.942** | **0.938** | **0.929** | **0.80** |

The drift follows P(W) = P∞ + C/W to better than 1e-4 at every intermediate point (C = 0.039 for A, 0.20 for D), which is exactly what sinc² tails along the *pump stripe* give: the pump envelope does not suppress the antidiagonal, so the 1/x² sinc tails integrate to a 1/W weight there, whichever way the ridge points. The asymmetric points drift ~4–5× more slowly than D — your Sec. 4.5 mechanism (ratio of phase-matching to pump width) correctly explains the *ratio* — but they are not converged, and "spread below 8×10⁻⁴" is an artifact of the narrow s-range. Note that Graffitti et al. (Optica 2018, your [graffitti2018optica]) already make this point explicitly: truncating the JSA to the central peak raised their apparent ppKTP purity from 80.1 % to 93.2 %.

What to do (this turns a weakness into one of the paper's better contributions):
1. Replace the "converged vs unconverged" dichotomy with the statement that *every* unfiltered sinc-limited purity is a function of the collection band, with a 1/W tail whose coefficient is 4–5× smaller for asymmetric GVM. Report C and P∞ for each featured point (a two-column addition to Table 2).
2. Quote the headline as "P = 0.949 within a ±110-nm collection band around 1364 nm (P∞ = 0.942 for unbounded collection)", or simply adopt a physically motivated band (detector or coupling bandwidth) and state it. Either is defensible; "converged" is not.
3. Keep, and emphasise, that the **filtered** purities (P90, P70) are window-independent — I confirm this to 2e-4 between 6σ and 16σ windows. They are the robust numbers, and the 90 %-transmission filter at A is a very ordinary 10.7-nm FWHM bandpass at 1364 nm. Say so in nm; the current text quotes filters only in rad/ps or as multiples of the pump width.
4. Fix Fig. 4 caption ("best window-converged unfiltered purity"), Table 1's four-decimal P column (false precision), Sec. 4.1's "0.935–0.954", and the Conclusions' "window-converged and truncation-limited" recommendation accordingly.

### M3. Prior art and positioning

1. **Jin et al. 2020** (arXiv:1912.02497 / Quantum Eng. 2, e38) report, for BiBO x–z type-II at degeneracy: GVM1 687 nm (46.0°, d_eff 2.50 pm/V, P ≈ 0.97), GVM2 1119 nm (46.0°, 2.30 pm/V, P ≈ 0.97), GVM3 875 nm (43.8°, 2.48 pm/V, P ≈ 0.82). These are your points A, C and D. The manuscript acknowledges the wavelengths and angles but is silent on (a) the purity comparison — your 0.949/0.939 vs their 0.97/0.97 — and (b) the d_eff discrepancy (their 2.50 vs your 2.22 at the same angle, i.e. a different d-tensor set). A referee will ask about both. Reproduce Jin's purities with Jin's L and bandwidth (and, per M2, their implied window) and state what the difference is; identify the d-coefficient set behind 2.50 pm/V.
2. **False sentence, Sec. 1**: "Every crystal surveyed in the works cited above is orthorhombic, tetragonal or uniaxial, so that d_eff is either even or odd in the polar angle and one octant suffices." Jin 2020 — cited two paragraphs earlier as containing BIBO's degenerate points — surveys monoclinic BiBO. Rewrite as a statement about *how* the monoclinic d_eff was handled (single octant, no y–z treatment) if that is what you can show; otherwise drop it.
3. **Missing BIBO-SPDC literature**: at least *Optimizing the phase-matching parameters of a BIBO crystal for ultrafast SPDC*, Proc. SPIE 8333 (2012); *Calculation of effective nonlinear coefficient in BIBO for spontaneous parametric down conversion* (c. 2014); *The factors of SPDC in BiB₃O₆ with a broadband pump*, Optik (2013). None is a GVM/purity survey, but they are the BIBO SPDC design literature and a referee from that community will notice their absence.
4. **State-of-the-art numbers are stale or misattributed** (Sec. 5.2): "95.3 % by 2021 [pickston2021]" — 95.3 % is Graffitti et al. 2018's *theoretical* estimate for their aKTP crystal; Pickston et al. 2021 report a *measured* lower bound of 98.0 ± 0.1 % (HOM visibility 98.6 %). Unfiltered domain-engineered sources in 2025 report 98.5–99.9 % (e.g. arXiv:2510.06196). The "bulk BIBO reaches without poling what poled crystals reach only with domain engineering" sentence should be rewritten around the honest gap: 0.94–0.95 unfiltered (or 0.988 with a 10 % flux penalty) vs ≥ 0.98 unfiltered for apodized KTP, in exchange for no poling and a different wavelength band.

## 4. Moderate issues

- **Sec. 3.2 is oversold.** "Degenerate GVM engineering requires a type-II interaction" is a one-line identity (same polarisation and wavelength ⇒ same k′), and the triple-GVM broadband type-I point is the well-known GVM-matched degenerate type-I configuration (it is how ultrabroadband biphoton sources are designed; cf. O'Donnell & U'Ren 2007, and the type-I/type-II discussion in Mosley et al., NJP 10, 093011 (2008)). Keep it — it is a useful organising remark and it explains Fig. 2 — but present it as a remark with a citation, not as one of "two structural results" in the abstract. Referees react badly to trivial results labelled theorems.
- **Purity optimum at the scan boundary.** At A, B and C the best L is 10 mm, the edge of your grid. LPR2010 lists BIBO boules of 20×20×30 mm³; extend the scan to 20–30 mm (with the M2 correction) so that the paper reports an optimum rather than a boundary value, and quantify the pump-bandwidth/L trade-off there.
- **Sinc ceiling value.** Text says 0.81–0.83, abstract says "never exceeds 0.86", Fig. 4 guide is 0.83, Sec. 4.2 says "0.83 ± 0.02". Meyer-Scott et al., PRA 95, 061803 (2017) (your paper 15) give 0.80 for Gaussian pump + sinc PMF. Harmonise, and note that after M2 the EPM family's P∞ is ≈ 0.80, i.e. exactly the literature value — a nicer validation than the current one.
- **Brightness proxy (Sec. 4.6).** Eq. (7) is a plane-wave proxy; say explicitly that it ignores the Boyd–Kleinman focusing dependence and single-mode collection, and that the "order of magnitude" for E is FoM² = (0.102/0.026)² ≈ 15. Also note that at E the walk-off is on the *pump* (e-wave) while at A–D it is on a *daughter*; these do not enter a collection geometry identically.
- **Table 2**: add filter FWHM in nm for P90/P70; add C (the 1/W coefficient) and P∞ per M2; point B does not appear in Fig. 1 although it is a featured point.
- **Fig. 5, panel R**: the white band at the bottom is the 3.05-µm grid clip. Either say so in the caption or drop R from the figure (it is a comparison point only).
- **Fig. 1**: the 0° oeo family (the headline family) is a barely visible 44-nm-wide segment; consider an inset or a second panel with an expanded pump axis.
- **Sec. 2.5, KTP remark** ("the popular Kato–Takaoka n_y ... misclassifies the GVM regime") is an unsupported aside in a BIBO paper; either give the number or cut it.
- **Data availability**: "available from the author upon reasonable request" for the code is weak for Optics Express; a Zenodo/GitHub DOI costs nothing and referees increasingly expect it for a paper whose contribution is computational.

## 6. Novelty and suitability for Optics Express

**What is genuinely new here.** (i) The continuous-pump, level-curve survey of all eight LPR2010 interactions with the full monoclinic d_eff in both octants — nobody has published the nondegenerate GVM loci of BIBO, and point B (722 nm Ti:sapphire pump → 1.156/1.924 µm, P ≈ 0.94) is a new, practical configuration. (ii) The three negative results over the full pump range (x–y useless; y–z carries the largest d_eff but no GVM; y–z oeo has d_eff ≡ 0). (iii) The purity-vs-heralding-flux curves (Fig. 6) — the literature quotes filtered purities without the flux cost far too often. (iv) After correction per M2, a clean quantitative statement of how much every unfiltered sinc-limited purity in this genre depends on the collection band, with the 1/W law and its coefficient — this applies to Jin's 0.97 as much as to your 0.949 and would be the paper's most transferable methodological point. (v) The Sellmeier-sensitivity table.

**What is not new.** The three degenerate x–z type-II points (A, C, D), their angles and the qualitative purity ranking (asymmetric ≫ EPM) are in Jin et al. 2020. The type-I remark is elementary. The EPM sinc ceiling is textbook. The transparency argument, as currently written, is wrong.

**Assessment.** As drafted, a well-informed referee would return "major revision" and could argue for rejection on novelty grounds by pointing at Jin 2020 and at M1. With M1–M3 fixed and the emphasis shifted toward (i), (iii) and (iv) — i.e. the paper sold as *the nondegenerate landscape plus a rigorous accounting of what "unfiltered purity" means*, with BIBO as the worked case — it is, in my judgement, within Optics Express's scope and novelty bar (the journal has published closely comparable numerical photon-pair design studies, e.g. Branczyk et al. 2011, and Hentschel et al. 2011 on BIBO itself; the closest analogues to this survey — Laudenbach 2017, Jin 2019 — went to Phys. Rev. Applied, which is also a realistic target). Optics Continuum or JOSA B would be lower-risk fallbacks if the OE editor pushes back on novelty. I would not recommend a higher-tier venue.

**Suggested order of work.** M1 (rewrite Sec. 5.1 and every downstream sentence; half a day) → M2 (add a real window scan to landscape_headline.py — vary the pad, not just the sinc term — fit 1/W, add two columns to Table 2, rewrite Sec. 4.5 and the abstract; one day) → M3 (reproduce Jin's numbers, fix the false sentence, add citations, update Sec. 5.2; half a day) → extend L to 30 mm → moderate/minor items.
