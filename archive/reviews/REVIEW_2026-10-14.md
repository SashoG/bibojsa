## A. Calculation correctness

**1. [fix before submission] At point C the heralding filter is applied to the broad arm (Table 2, Fig. 6, Sec. 4.2, Sec. 5.3 (ii)).**
`landscape_points.py:101`, `landscape_headline.py:88`, `window_tail.py:75` always filter the idler axis, and tex:425 says so ("Gaussian amplitude filter on the idler"). At A and B (θ_PM = 0°, k_p' = k_s') the ridge is horizontal and the idler is the narrow arm, so this is right. At C (θ_PM = 90°, k_p' = k_i') the ridge is vertical — the Fig. 5 caption itself says "C is the same picture transposed" — so the narrow arm is the *signal* (marginal FWHM 2.45 rad/ps vs 23.65 for the idler), and an idler filter only clips the pump-limited broad marginal. Recomputed with the package JSA on the paper's window (10 mm, 16-nm pump):

| C, filter on | T = 90 % | T = 70 % |
|---|---|---|
| idler (Table 2 as printed) | P = 0.963, 143 nm | P = 0.982, 66 nm |
| signal (narrow arm) | P = 0.984, 28.8 nm | P = 0.993, 9.9 nm |

(For comparison A: idler 0.988/10.6 nm vs signal 0.968/70 nm; B: idler 0.985 vs signal 0.964; D symmetric, 0.918 either way.) The printed C numbers are not wrong for an idler filter, but they are not comparable with A and B, they understate C, and they contradict the paper's own rule at B (tex:1132–1135: herald on the narrow arm, otherwise "forfeit the cheap filtering"). Fix: filter the narrower arm ("heralding arm") at every point, regenerate the C column of Table 2 (P90, P70, Δλ90, Δλ70) and the C curve of Fig. 6, and reword tex:425 ("on the narrow-band heralding arm — the idler at A, B, D, R, the signal at C"), the Sec. 4.2 sentence "the filter removes the idler-side tails and the pump envelope the signal-side ones" (true at A/B only), and tex:1128 ("Point C heralds at 2.234 µm" → "on its e-polarized signal at 2.234 µm"). Sec. 4.2's "the asymmetric points are cheap to improve" then holds for all three.

**2. [low] Walk-off factor in `pair_rate.py` (tex:1038–1041).** The script uses (L_eff/L)² with a two-equal-Gaussian overlap exp(−(ρz)²/2W²). The spectrally integrated rate goes as (1/L)∫h²dz (Parseval), and with only the e-daughter displaced the three-beam overlap is exp(−2(ρz)²/3W²). The two corrections nearly cancel at 10 mm/0.48 mm (0.427 → 0.432; "~0.4×10³" stands) but not at 1 mm/30 µm (0.19 → 0.27): "3–5×10³ with walk-off" becomes about 3.3–6.8×10³. The 20–1400× range is unaffected. Optional: write "3–7×10³". The estimate is already labelled indicative.

**3. [info] `pair_rate.py:68–77` "sanity check" against Ling's BBO experiment is not a validation** — that measurement was non-collinear (3.1°), so the 1.7× gap says nothing about the collinear formula. The formula itself was re-derived from H_I = 4ε₀ d EEE and the exact sinc² integral and is implemented verbatim; the group-index replacement for (n_i − n_s) is the correct one. Just do not cite the BBO comparison as a check (EXPLORATION md, Sec. 2).

**4. [info, before Zenodo release] Unit labels.** `offplane_degenerate_loci.csv` *and* `offplane_epm_locus.csv` (contrary to the EXPLORATION note) carry ps/mm in `*_fs_mm` columns. The manuscript values (169→174, 166→189 fs/mm) are correct. `landscape_degenerate.csv` lists θ_PM = 135.0 for the type-I rows where the text says −45° (equivalent mod 180°; `gvm.theta_pm_deg` docstring says (−90, 90] but arctan2 returns (−180, 180]). Normalise before the data release.

**5. [minor text] "C four to six times smaller" (abstract, Sec. 4.4, conclusions).** From Table 2, C_D/C_C = 0.21/0.056 = 3.8 and C_D/C_B = 0.21/0.038 = 5.5. "Four to five times" or "roughly four to five" is what the numbers say; "four to six" rounds up at both ends.

## C. Figures

The two palettes collide: `cg0` (θ_PM = 0°) and `coeo` (oeo) are the same blue, `cg90` and `cooe` nearly the same green. In Fig. 3 the left panel colours interactions and the right panel colours GVM conditions with the *same* colours, so blue means oeo on the left and 0° on the right. Give the interaction palette distinct hues (or grey levels) from the GVM-condition palette.

**Fig. 1.** The x-axis 0.62–1.16 µm is shared, but the type-I families span only 0.65–0.90 µm, so the two right panels are half empty and their near-coincident curves are compressed into a quarter of the panel width. Give the type-I panels their own x-range (0.64–0.92 µm) — the shared axis buys nothing because the point of those panels is coincidence, not comparison with oeo. ymin 0.75 could be 0.80 (no data below 0.83 µm).

**Fig. 2.** Fine as is; still the first candidate to drop if space is needed (the type-I lines carry one bit: −45° everywhere).

**Fig. 3, right panel.** Nine curves, three line styles, no way to tell oeo from ooe from eeo (e.g. the two blue 0° curves at 0.68–0.72 and 0.66–0.82 µm). Add text labels on the curves (oeo / ooe / eeo) as in the left panel, or use the interaction palette for colour and θ_PM for line style in both panels. Log axis and range are good.

**Fig. 4.** Three problems, all fixable in the .tex (proposed version: `manuscript/review_2026-10-14/fig4_proposed.tex/.pdf`):
(a) The oeo panel's data occupy the top 20 % of a 0–1 axis; all the paper's headline comparisons (0.77–0.954 vs the 0.83 guide) live in that strip. Plot the oeo panel on 0.70–1.0 and say "note the different vertical scale" in the caption; the type-I panels keep 0–1.
(b) The caption has to *tell* the reader that the eeo 0° points above the guide have idlers beyond 2.6 µm, but `purity_*.csv` already carries the `inwin` flag — plot members with λ_i > 2.6 µm as open markers (pgfplots `scatter/classes` on `meta=inwin`). This also reveals, in the oeo panel, that the 0.954 maximum and the whole short-pump half of the EPM family are open markers, which is exactly Sec. 5.1's point.
(c) The ooe 0° curve zigzags (0.21, 0.20, 0.17, 0.15, 0.03, 0.13, 0.24, 0.23, …) because the (L, bandwidth) optimum of a near-triple JSA jumps between grid corners; the connecting lines suggest a trend that is not there and the eeo 0° line draws a vertical drop at the triple point. Plot the type-I panels as markers only. The type-I x-range can also shrink to 0.64–0.92 µm as in Fig. 1.

**Fig. 5.** (a) Panel R has a white rectangle below δω_i ≈ −55 rad/ps (the idler grid stops at the 3.05-µm Sellmeier edge). The caption explains it, but it reads as a rendering error; crop the panel to the valid region (`ymin=-55`, keep xmin/xmax) and say "asymmetric window" in the caption, or fill the region with the colormap's zero. (b) Panels A and B: a ±4 rad/ps ridge inside a ±70/±80 window; with the pump FWHM at 32 rad/ps the ridge content ends at about ±40. A ±45 window keeps equal scales (the caption's reason for the choice) and doubles the ridge's apparent size. (c) The moiré-like stripes along the ridges in A/B/C are resolved sinc side-lobes at ~4 pixels per lobe; acceptable, but a 200-cell mesh for A–C only would remove the visual aliasing at small cost (lualatex already required).

**Fig. 6.** This is the figure whose axis hides its content: A–D and R all sit in 0.8–1.0 while E uses the rest of the axis, and the quantities the text argues about (0.949 → 0.988 → 0.994; D overtaking the asymmetric points at 70 %) are a few pixels. Plot the impurity 1 − P on a log axis (proposed version: `fig6_proposed.tex/.pdf`, range 0.003–1): the A/B curves separate from C and D, the D–A crossing near 65 % transmission that Sec. 4.2 describes becomes visible, and E stays in frame. Once item 1 is fixed, the C curve moves down to the A/B pair. Keep the P90/P70 verticals; the legend entry for D in the mock-up lost its colour (cosmetic).
