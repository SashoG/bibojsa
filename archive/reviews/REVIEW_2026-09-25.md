## A. Statements contradicted by the paper's own data

**1. "The asymmetric oeo families are the only places where the unfiltered purity exceeds the symmetric-GVM sinc value" (Sec. 4.1) — false, and visible in Fig. 4.**
- `family_purity.csv`: the eeo 0° family reaches 0.890 / 0.881 / 0.870 / 0.862 / 0.856 / 0.847 / 0.841 at 836–864 nm pump. That is above the 0.8315 dotted "sinc ceiling" line, and Fig. 4's right panel shows it.
- `points2_summary.csv` #37: x–z eoe 0° at 642 nm gives 0.951 (edge 0.015).
- All of these have idlers at 2.64–2.92 µm, beyond the 2.6-µm absorption onset. So the claim holds only with a qualifier: "among members whose idler lies below the absorption onset" (and eoe is excluded on d_eff anyway).

**2. Type-I is always "near-triple" (Sec. 3.2, Fig. 4 caption) — not true at the eeo 0° end.**
- The text says every nondegenerate type-I GVM point has a phase-matching bandwidth "far broader than any practical pump" and that "the pump envelope dominates".
- Along the eeo 0° family, however, the group-delay scale is 29–48 fs/mm (836–878 nm). That is the same size as the oeo 45° (24–73) and 90° (37–166) families, and those points reach P = 0.81–0.89.
- The stated dichotomy ranges overlap: intra-branch 0.5–**54** fs/mm vs inter-branch **37**–189 fs/mm.
- The Fig. 4 caption's "the low values in the two type-I panels are the near-triple effect" is also untrue for the eeo 0° branch.
- Suggested fix: scope the argument to near-degenerate daughters. Say that the intra-branch mismatch grows with daughter separation, so the only type-I members with type-II-like scales have idlers at 2.5–2.9 µm.

**3. "The optimum pump bandwidth scales as 1/L" (Sec. 4.1) — own data say ≈ L^−1/2.**
- `length_extension.csv` optima for 10 → 15 → 20 → 30 mm: A 8 → 6 → 6 → 4 nm; B 12 → 8 → 8 → 6; C 16 → 16 → 12 → 12. Over 3× in L the bandwidth drops by 2× (A, B) or 1.33× (C), not 3×.
- This fits physically: at Δ32 = 0 the signal-arm width is set by GVD, which scales as L^−1/2.
- Consequence for the Jin comparison: the sentence "a 3-nm pump corresponds to L ≈ 25–30 mm, where we find 0.967" is wrong. At 30 mm a 3-nm pump gives 0.962, below the 0.967 of a 4-nm pump. Under L^−1/2 scaling, 3 nm is optimal near 70 mm, which is inside Jin's 4–50-mm range only at its top end. Rewrite that sentence.
- The same wording ("optimum pump bandwidth scales as 1/L") should not be used to justify anything else. Sec. 4.5's "total pair rate falls as 1/L" is a separate claim and is fine.

**4. Jin 2020 d_eff comparison — the stated explanation does not work numerically (Sec. 2.5).**
- Jin 2.50 / 2.48 / 2.30 against ours 2.22 / 2.31 / 2.22 gives ratios 1.126 / 1.074 / 1.036. These are not constant. The claimed cause (3.48 vs 3.2 pm/V) would give a constant 1.0875.
- 3.48·cos θ at Jin's angles gives 2.42 / 2.51 / 2.41, which does not match Jin's values either.
- Also, the manuscript's own x–z oeo expression is d12 cos θ, not d26.
- Fix: say that the values differ by 4–13% and that the Kleinman spread (Sec. 2.2) covers this, or find Jin's actual d-tensor and dispersion (Miller scaling?) before attributing the difference.

**5. "The entire nondegenerate EPM family … is in the same position" (Sec. 5.1, i.e., idler beyond 2.6 µm) — false.**
29 of the 62 traced oeo 45° points carry `lambda_i_in_transparency = 1`. These are the 840–882 nm members (D included), with idlers at 1.74–2.59 µm (`landscape_curves.csv`). Only the short-pump half (774–838 nm), which includes R, is beyond the onset. The same paragraph also said "all featured configurations lie below it", which is false for R (Table 2 itself flags R "no").

**6. Off-plane numbers: conclusions vs Sec. 5.3 (i).**
- The conclusions say "improves by at most 10%". Sec. 5.3 (i) and the exploration give 11–12% (A +12%, D +11%, C +12%). Use "about 10%" or "11–12%" in both places.
- "|Δ31| and the purity are unchanged (169 to 174 fs/mm; P = 0.949 to 0.951)" applies to the A locus only. On the C locus the group-delay scale goes 166 → **189** fs/mm (+14%) per `offplane_degenerate_loci.csv`, and purity was checked only on A.
- Add "on the A locus".
- Internal note: `EXPLORATION_2026-09-21.md` also lists "166 → 174" for C. That is wrong; its own CSV and `landscape_degenerate.csv` (y–z oeo 90°, 189.3 fs/mm) give 189.

## C. Medium

**8. Pair-rate sentence (Sec. 4.5) mixes bases.**
- "~1×10³ … before walk-off (0.4×10³ with it)" is followed by "3–5×10³ for a 1-mm crystal at 30–60 µm". Those are the after-walk-off values; before walk-off they are 6.3–25×10³ (`pair_rate_output.txt`).
- "An order of magnitude below 25 kHz/mW" fits the 1-mm case (5–8×) but not 10 mm (25–60×).
- Also state whether kim2025's 25.1 kHz/mW is a detected or an inferred generated rate. The comparison is against a generated-into-mode prediction.

**9. Data availability is stale.** It still says Data File 1 and "code on reasonable request". The in-source TODO records the July decision "no external DOI", but that decision was reversed on 22 Sep (Zenodo DOI, public repo SashoG/bibojsa). All six captions cite "(Data File 1)". Update once the DOI exists.

**10. x–y eoe range (Sec. 3.1).**
- The text gives "335–396 nm" and θ_PM "−21.4° to −19.5°". `landscape_field.csv` gives 340–395 nm and −21.38° to −19.71°.
- 335 nm lies outside the stated 0.34–1.52 µm sweep, and the 340-nm lower end is the grid edge, not a physical cutoff.
- A 1-nm re-scan with bibojsa (added when applying the fixes) gives phase matching from 330 nm (the edge of the dispersion data as used) to 396 nm, with θ_PM from −21.4° to −19.4°.

**11. Grid-resolution claim (Sec. 4.4).** "Halving the pixel changes every purity in Table 2 by < 3×10⁻⁵" exists only as a code comment in `window_tail.py`. The deposited `headline_convergence.csv`, at fixed window, shows N-dependence up to 2.4×10⁻⁴ (D) and 4.1×10⁻⁴ (R), and 5.7×10⁻⁵ (A) and 7.4×10⁻⁵ (C). Either deposit the pixel test or restate the bound (it is < 5×10⁻⁴, which is still harmless to 3 decimals).

**12. Optima at grid edges.**
- Every asymmetric-family optimum in Fig. 4 sits at L = 10 mm, the top of the 0.5–10-mm grid.
- E and F sit at 32 nm, the top of the bandwidth grid.
- A at 0.5 mm (Sec. 4.5) is at 32 nm, so 0.795 is a lower bound.
- Say "at the 10-mm cap" in the Fig. 4 caption.
- Sec. 4.3 says "0.1–10 mm" while Sec. 4 says 0.5–10 mm.

## F. Resolution (v0.8, 25 Sep 2026)

All items above were applied to `manuscript_oe.tex` v0.8 (header change log lists them). Two findings changed while the fixes were being made:

- **Item 8.** kim2025's 25.1 kHz/mW is a *detected* coincidence rate (20-mm KTP, SNSPDs about 40% efficient at 1079 nm) at a 21% coincidence-to-singles ratio (arXiv:2409.07673). The v0.7 text said "an order of magnitude", so the gap was understated. Sec. 4.5 now says this.
