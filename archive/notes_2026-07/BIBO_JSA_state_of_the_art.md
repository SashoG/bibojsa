# Spectrally engineered photon pairs from BiB₃O₆: state of the art and proposed contribution

*Working document — draft background/introduction material and literature map. V0.1, July 2026.*

---

## 1. Motivation

Heralded single photons and entangled photon pairs generated by spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) remain the workhorse of photonic quantum information: multiphoton entanglement, boson sampling, measurement-device-independent QKD, and photonic quantum computing demonstrations all rely on them. Two figures of merit dominate source design beyond raw brightness: (i) the **spectral purity** of the heralded photon, which bounds the visibility of quantum interference between independent sources, and (ii) the **entanglement quality** (state fidelity) for pair sources.

Both are governed by the same object — the **joint spectral amplitude (JSA)** of the biphoton — and the JSA is in turn fully determined by classical nonlinear-optics quantities: the pump spectral envelope and the phase-matching function of the crystal, i.e. Sellmeier equations, group velocities, effective nonlinearity, and geometry. "JSA engineering" is therefore phase-matching engineering viewed through a quantum-information lens. This is exactly the machinery developed for BIBO in the femtosecond frequency-conversion literature [1–4], which has never been systematically translated into the quantum-source design language. That translation is the subject of the proposed work.

## 2. Formalism: JSA, Schmidt decomposition, purity

To first order in perturbation theory, the SPDC biphoton state is

$$|\psi\rangle = \iint d\omega_s\, d\omega_i\; f(\omega_s,\omega_i)\; \hat a_s^\dagger(\omega_s)\, \hat a_i^\dagger(\omega_i)\, |0\rangle,$$

with the JSA factorizing into pump envelope and phase-matching function:

$$f(\omega_s,\omega_i) = \alpha(\omega_s+\omega_i)\cdot \phi(\omega_s,\omega_i), \qquad \phi \propto \mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\tfrac{\Delta k(\omega_s,\omega_i)\,L}{2}\right) e^{i\Delta k L/2}.$$

The Schmidt decomposition $f(\omega_s,\omega_i)=\sum_n \sqrt{\lambda_n}\, u_n(\omega_s)\, v_n(\omega_i)$ [5] quantifies spectral entanglement via the Schmidt number $K = 1/\sum_n \lambda_n^2$; the purity of the heralded single photon is $P = \sum_n \lambda_n^2 = 1/K$. A **factorable** JSA ($K=1$, $P=1$) yields heralded photons in pure states that interfere with unit visibility between independent sources without spectral filtering [6, 7].

Geometric intuition (standard in the field, and the design target of everything below): in the $(\omega_s,\omega_i)$ plane, $\alpha$ is a band along the anti-diagonal $\omega_s+\omega_i=\omega_p$; $\phi$ is a band whose orientation angle $\theta_{PM}$ is set by the group-velocity mismatch (GVM) parameters,

$$\tan\theta_{PM} = -\frac{k_p' - k_s'}{k_p' - k_i'}, \qquad k_x' \equiv \frac{\partial k}{\partial\omega}\bigg|_{\omega_x} = \frac{1}{v_{g,x}}.$$

Factorability requires the product of the two bands to be separable, which happens for specific GVM conditions:

- **Asymmetric GVM** ($v_{g,p} = v_{g,s}$ or $v_{g,p} = v_{g,i}$, $\theta_{PM}=0°$ or $90°$): the phase-matching band lies parallel to one frequency axis. One photon is spectrally broad, the other narrow. Classic case: type-II KDP pumped at 415 nm [7, 8].
- **Symmetric GVM / extended phase matching (EPM)** ($k_p' = (k_s'+k_i')/2$, $\theta_{PM}=45°$): with a pump bandwidth matched to the phase-matching bandwidth, the JSA becomes circular. Classic case: type-II KTP/PPKTP around 1582 nm [9–11].

Note the direct connection to the classical femtosecond literature: $\theta_{PM}=45°$ is precisely the condition of vanishing GVM between pump and the signal/idler pair — the same $\Delta_{21},\Delta_{31},\Delta_{32}$ parameters tabulated for BIBO in [1] (Figs. 9–13) and exploited there for broadband OPA. Table 5 of [1] ("magic" pump wavelengths, $1/v_P - 1/v_{S,I}$) is, unrecognized at the time, a table of candidate EPM operating points for quantum light.

A hard limitation of bulk birefringent crystals: even at the ideal GVM point, the sinc sidelobes of $\phi$ limit purity to $P \approx 0.80{-}0.85$ without filtering [12, 13]. The state of the art divides according to how this ceiling is attacked.

## 3. State of the art in JSA/purity engineering

### 3.1 Group-velocity matching in bulk birefringent crystals

The foundational theory of spectral correlations in SPDC and their elimination is due to Grice, U'Ren and Walmsley [6, 14, 15]. Experimentally, Mosley et al. demonstrated heralded pure ultrafast single photons from bulk KDP at the asymmetric GVM point (pump 415 nm, photons at 830 nm), achieving ~94% interference visibility between independent sources without spectral filtering [7]. Systematic surveys of GVM wavelengths in common bulk crystals (BBO, KDP, KTP family, LiIO₃, etc.) exist [8, 15]; each uniaxial or QPM material typically offers the condition at one or few discrete wavelengths, which rarely coincide with technologically convenient pump lasers — the same "magic wavelength" mismatch problem known from ultrabroadband OPA design [1].

### 3.2 Extended phase matching and quasi-phase-matched engineering

König and Wong identified the EPM condition in PPKTP [9]; Kuzucu et al. and later Evans et al. exploited it for spectrally uncorrelated, bright polarization-entangled pairs near 1580 nm [10, 11]. PPKTP at telecom wavelengths, usually in a Sagnac loop [16], has since become the de facto standard source.

Because QPM crystals allow the *nonlinearity profile itself* to be modulated along the propagation axis, the sinc-sidelobe ceiling can be removed by **apodization / domain engineering**: Brańczyk et al. introduced custom poling for Gaussian phase-matching functions [12]; Dosseva et al. optimized domain sequences by simulated annealing [17]; Graffitti et al. developed sub-coherence-length domain engineering delivering purities >0.99 and demonstrated it experimentally [13, 18, 19]. Machine-learning frameworks now design poling profiles achieving computed purities of 99.2–99.99% across 1310–1600 nm in KTP [20], and current work continues on equal-width robust poling patterns [21], pump-plus-nonlinearity co-shaping [22], and application-specific optimization (telecom O-band [23], time-multiplexed sources at 900–950 nm [24]). Reviews: [25, 26].

**Key point for positioning BIBO:** none of the apodization machinery is available in bulk birefringent crystals — BIBO cannot be poled. A bulk-crystal proposal must therefore (a) find natural GVM/EPM operating points at useful wavelengths, (b) rely on pump shaping, crystal length, and geometry for the residual sidelobe problem, and (c) compensate with material advantages: BIBO's $d_{\text{eff}}$ (up to ~3.7 pm/V in the $y$–$z$ plane [1, 27]) exceeds BBO's by roughly a factor 1.5–2 and rivals periodically poled materials' *effective usable* nonlinearity once damage, hygroscopicity, aperture, and cost are accounted for. Pair-generation rate scales as $d_{\text{eff}}^2$, which is why BiBO already displaced BBO in multiphoton experiments (Sec. 4).

### 3.3 Pump shaping and hybrid schemes

Complementary to crystal engineering: shaping the pump spectrum (non-Gaussian envelopes, sech² vs Gaussian trade-offs) directly sculpts the anti-diagonal band of the JSA [22, 28]. For bulk crystals this — together with modest filtering of the *heralding* arm only — is the main practical knob and must be part of our analysis.

### 3.4 The biaxial precedent: BMBF

The closest methodological template for our work is the 2020 numerical study of Ba₃Mg₃(BO₃)₃F₃ (BMBF, orthorhombic mm2) [29]. The authors developed the full EPM theory for bulk *biaxial* crystals — noting explicitly that it is "much more complex than the cases of uniaxial crystals and periodically poled crystals" — and found that, unlike PPKTP where EPM occurs at a single wavelength, the biaxial degrees of freedom let the EPM condition be satisfied *continuously over spectral ranges* (876–1053 nm pump for type I, 884–914 nm for type II), with computed heralded purities up to 0.997 with pump filtering and 0.833 unfiltered. This is the decisive conceptual point: **lower crystal symmetry converts discrete magic wavelengths into tunable magic curves.** BIBO (monoclinic, point group 2) is the lowest-symmetry practical NLO crystal in use, with an additional angular degree of freedom relative to orthorhombic crystals [1, 27] — the EPM loci should be correspondingly richer. No one has computed them.

Notably, [29] dismisses BIBO in its comparison section on the grounds of its ~40 mrad spatial walk-off limiting usable crystal length. This objection is quantitative, not fundamental, and answering it (Sec. 5, item iii) is part of the contribution.

## 4. BIBO in quantum optics: current status

What exists:

1. **Characterization as an entangled-pair source.** Halevy et al. fully characterized non-collinear type-II SPDC in BiBO for polarization entanglement — cut angles, photon polarizations, $d_{\text{eff}}$, spatial/temporal walk-offs, crystal thickness, pump-bandwidth effects — and demonstrated generation rates enhanced over BBO as if pump power had been increased [30]. Earlier, the Kwiat group produced polarization entanglement directly from BiBO in type-I sandwich configurations [31].
2. **Multiphoton experiments.** The USTC group's ten-photon entanglement used thin BiBO crystals precisely for the brightness advantage, and stated openly that the pair from non-collinear type-II BiBO is *not* an ideal Bell state ($\cos(7\pi/30)|HH\rangle + \sin(7\pi/30)|VV\rangle$) and that the two SPDC-ring intersections have *different spectral widths* — engineering problems left to per-experiment compensation [32].
3. **Acceptance-level phase-matching analyses.** Two theoretical papers optimized SPDC phase-matching parameters in BIBO (type I at 405 nm pump [33]) and computed walk-offs, acceptance angles and spectral acceptance under broadband pumping, concluding type II is preferable [34]. These stop at acceptance bandwidths — no JSA, no Schmidt decomposition, no purity, no GVM/EPM loci.
4. **Peripheral uses.** BiBO appears in engineering-tolerance studies of entangled sources alongside PPKTP [35], and in UV enhancement cavities for multiphoton pumping [36].

What does **not** exist (verified against the literature to mid-2026):

- No map of asymmetric-GVM and symmetric-GVM/EPM loci for BIBO in its principal planes and out-of-plane, for any interaction type.
- No JSA / Schmidt-mode / heralded-purity computation for any BIBO configuration.
- No treatment of how the monoclinic point-group-2 symmetry (deff neither even nor odd in θ; two inequivalent octants; interaction-type/plane coupling — see Table 1 and Figs. 6–7 of [1]) enriches or constrains the factorability conditions.
- No quantitative answer to the walk-off objection of [29] in the quantum-source context.

## 5. Proposed contribution

Working title: *"Group-velocity-matched photon-pair generation in the monoclinic crystal BiB₃O₆: joint spectral engineering, heralded-photon purity, and comparison with uniaxial and quasi-phase-matched sources."*

**(i) Complete GVM/EPM cartography.** Using the Sellmeier equations of Miyata–Umemura–Kato [37] (Table 2 of [1], valid 326–3083 nm), solve the phase-matching and GVM conditions for all interaction types (I, II, III) in the three principal planes and for out-of-plane propagation (formalism of Tzankov–Petrov [27]), for SPDC pumped at practically relevant wavelengths: 405 nm (GaN diodes), 515 nm (SHG of Yb), 775–780 nm (SHG of Er-fiber / Ti:sapphire → telecom C-band pairs), 800–810 nm. Deliverables: loci of $\theta_{PM}=0°, 45°, 90°$ in the $(\lambda_p, \lambda_s)$ plane per configuration, with $|d_{\text{eff}}|$ along each locus. Immediate hook from the classical literature: for 800 nm pumping, signal and idler near $\lambda \approx 1340$ nm co-propagate with the pump group velocity in type-I ooe BIBO ([1], Sec. 4 and Fig. 10b) — an EPM-like point sitting at a Ti:sapphire-compatible pump that nobody has examined for biphoton factorability.

**(ii) JSA and purity maps.** For each candidate operating point: compute the JSA, Schmidt spectrum, heralded purity $P$, and Schmidt number $K$ versus crystal length, pump bandwidth, and pump-envelope shape (Gaussian/sech²); unfiltered and with heralding-arm filtering; benchmark methodology against the BMBF study [29] and the KTP standards [11, 13]. Include the non-collinear type-II configuration of [30, 32] to quantify (and propose fixes for) the non-ideal Bell coefficients and asymmetric ring spectra reported by the ten-photon experiment.

**(iii) Walk-off and geometry.** Quantify the walk-off-limited effective length at each operating point; analyze compensation: crossed/paired crystal geometries (standard in Kwiat-type sandwich sources [31]), thin-crystal stacks with relative orientation (the multiphoton community's de facto solution [32]), and beam-size optimization. Goal: show under what conditions BIBO's $d_{\text{eff}}^2$ advantage survives the length limitation — a direct rebuttal-with-numbers to [29].

**(iv) Comparison table.** BIBO vs BBO, KDP, bulk KTP, PPKTP (apodized and unapodized), BMBF: purity ceiling, brightness proxy ($d_{\text{eff}}^2 L_{\text{eff}}$), operating-wavelength flexibility, damage threshold, hygroscopicity, cost/aperture.

Everything in (i)–(iv) is computable from published dispersion data — no laboratory required. Natural experimental partners for a follow-up validation exist in the original BIBO network (Sofia University, MBI Berlin, ICFO) and in the quantum-optics groups already using BiBO [30, 32].
